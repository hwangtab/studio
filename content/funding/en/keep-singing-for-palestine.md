## On 19 September, we sang in the street

![Keep Singing for Peace thank-you poster — inside a CD-shaped frame, a group photo of participants holding banners in front of the stone wall at Seosipjagak-teo; below it, a note that the rally is over and details of the album reward](/images/funding/keep-singing-for-palestine/poster-20260927.webp)

Keep Singing for Peace, a street rally singing against troop dispatch and for the liberation of Palestine, ended safely.

That day the Gangjeong Peace and Music Camp organising committee joined an anti-dispatch rally in Jongno at 1 pm and gave a solidarity performance, and at 3 pm joined the Climate Justice March at Seoul City Hall. Then, from 6 pm, we held a three-hour rally with music at Seosipjagak-teo (서십자각터), in front of the stone wall of Gyeongbokgung Palace.

The people on stage and the staff, those who spoke, whether as invited speakers or from the open mic, and those who stopped as they passed by to cheer us on: together they made that time.

This is how we want to remember the day.

The world is burning with war and climate disaster, and the cartel of capital and militarism, together with nationalism, endlessly fragments people's lives. Even so, holding each other's hands, singing together, dancing and playing, we felt once again that all of us are capable of solidarity: of feeling other people's pain and of welcoming one another's existence.

Together we called out:

> US and Israel, stop the war of aggression and the genocide in West Asia!
>
> We reject military intervention in any form. No to backdoor troop dispatch!
>
> Unwavering solidarity in struggle: win the liberation of Palestine!

## What is the Gangjeong Peace and Music Camp?

In 2007, Gangjeong Village in Seogwipo, Jeju, was chosen as the site for a naval base. It was a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve and home to protected natural monuments. Nine years of fierce resistance and state violence followed. In that time more than 700 people were arrested, more than 60 were jailed, and fines of ₩400,000,000 and an indemnity claim of ₩3,450,000,000 were imposed. The base was completed in 2016, but the resistance did not stop.

Activists in Gangjeong still campaign every day for the closure of the Jeju Naval Base: the Life and Peace 100 Bows in front of the base at 7 am, a mass on the road at 11 am, and a human-chain cultural gathering at noon. This is the 19th year.

As this on-the-ground peace movement carried on, the 1st Gangjeong Peace and Music Camp was held in 2023: a festival carrying anti-war and peace messages through music, a language everyone can share. In 2024 we released the compilation album To You in a Distant Place Whose Name I Don't Know and held a release concert. After the 2nd camp in 2025, this year we held a successful 3rd camp over three days and two nights, with 50 musical acts.

The Gangjeong Peace and Music Camp aims for activity and solidarity beyond any narrow scope. Last May, on the block next to the Israeli embassy in Seoul, we held WE SING FOR YOUR FREEDOM, a cultural gathering in solidarity with Palestine. One line we called out that day sums up why, in a few words:

> From Palestine to the Korean Peninsula, as long as war does not end; from Gangjeong to Gaza, as long as not everyone is free, our song will not end either.

If South Korea decides to send troops to the US and Israeli war of aggression against Iran, the warships will set out from the Jeju Naval Base in Gangjeong Village, the only base in South Korea run on the premise of carrying out operations on the far seas. That is all the more reason we must raise our voices.

## What we send you — To You in a Distant Place Whose Name I Don't Know

![Back of To You in a Distant Place Whose Name I Don't Know — the CD sleeve lists the number, artist and title of all 13 tracks](/images/funding/keep-singing-for-palestine/tracklist-20260927.webp)

In 2024 the Gangjeong Peace and Music Camp could not hold the camp. Instead, 12 musical acts came together to make an album. Rock, folk, jazz and electronic music all went onto one record, 13 tracks in all including a hidden track.

The title, "To You in a Distant Place Whose Name I Don't Know" (이름을 모르는 먼 곳의 그대에게), names no particular recipient. In Iran, in Gaza, on the Korean Peninsula, there is someone whose face and name we don't know, and the album is a letter to that person.

Track 7, "We will sail for your freedom", was first written in 2016 by Emma Ringqvist for the women of the Gaza Strip. It is also a song made together by thirteen women activists who were sailing a boat to blockaded Gaza. Moredotoyoil sang it again.

The album closes with the hidden track "모르는" ("Not Knowing"). It is an autobiographical song written by two people who lived in Gangjeong for four years, in the middle of the movement against the naval base, and it calmly sings a day in the life of people who keep going one step at a time down a road they cannot see ahead. That song holds the reason this album, of all things, is the reward for this campaign.

## The second album — Fish Die Without Water

![Fish Die Without Water album cover — a single fish drawn on a dark purple background, with the album title handwritten in the lower right](/images/funding/keep-singing-for-palestine/fish-album-20260930.webp)

In 2022, the Noryangjin Fish Market Art Liberation Front (노량진수산시장 예술해방전선) released a compilation album, Fish Die Without Water (물고기는 물이 없으면 죽어요). It holds 8 songs by 8 musical acts standing in solidarity with the merchants evicted from the old Noryangjin Fish Market, songs written under the influence of the merchants' stories or made to be heard by them. Only 100 CDs were made, for the Seoul Record Fair, and there was no second pressing. All proceeds from those CD sales went to the merchants of the old Noryangjin Fish Market.

The title puts the merchants' feelings directly into words: just as fish die without water, people cannot make a living once the market, the ground of their lives, is taken away.

### What happened at the old Noryangjin Fish Market

Suhyup (the National Federation of Fisheries Cooperatives) built a new market and demanded that the merchants move. The merchants who stayed in the old market could not move because of higher rents and smaller selling space, and many of them were elderly women for whom moving was especially hard. According to a July 2018 report, about 270 merchants remained in the old market.

Court-ordered evictions began in April 2017, and in November 2018 the water and electricity were cut off. At the fifth eviction attempt on 25 April 2019, about 1,000 merchants and members of civic groups blocked it, and nine companies of police were sent in. Merchants fell to the ground and were injured, and two Suhyup employees were also taken to hospital. That August most of the stalls were demolished, and the evicted merchants pitched tents in front of Noryangjin Station and on the footbridge leading to the station, and began a sit-in.

Those years left their mark in numbers, too. In a 2019 survey answered by 61 merchants, 62.8% were in the at-risk group for depression and 69.4% said they had experienced hwabyeong, a Korean anger syndrome. In November 2021, one merchant who had kept up the sit-in died at the age of 53.

### Why the musicians went to the footbridge

The Art Liberation Front is a group formed in October 2019 by artists who had witnessed the violent ordeal of the merchants, most of them elderly women. Since then they have played music on the plaza at Noryangjin Station and made songs from the merchants' stories. They held cultural events inside the old market and kept up gatherings where artists standing in solidarity with the struggle could meet. This album is the record of those three years.

## Poems and songs — Park Chichi's Baljak and Gangdo

Two books collecting the poems and songs of Park Chichi (박치치). Park Chichi is a musician who also appears on Fish Die Without Water with "마지막 인사" ("Last Goodbye"). Both books contain poems and songs side by side.

![Cover of Baljak, Park Chichi's first book of poems and songs — on a white background, the title 발작, the author's name 박치치 and the words "first book of poems/songs"](/images/funding/keep-singing-for-palestine/book-baljak-20260928.webp)

**Baljak (발작, "Seizure", 2022)** is Park Chichi's first book of poems and songs. In days when the whole world was a question, the author says, they wrote down only wrong answers, every time. It gathers sentences from before they became songs and cries from before they became poems: 28 poems and 9 songs.

![Cover of Gangdo, Park Chichi's second book of poems and songs](/images/funding/keep-singing-for-palestine/book-gangdo-20260928.webp)

**Gangdo (갱도, "Mine Shaft", 2024)** is the second book of poems and songs. It is dedicated to every poem lost and sobbing in that dead-dark mine shaft, on a road that stays black however far you walk. It holds 25 poems and 7 songs.
