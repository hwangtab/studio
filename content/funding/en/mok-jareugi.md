## 10 October, in front of the trees still standing

![The Mok-jareugi concert poster — a person's neck, flushed red, laid over a green forest of Korean pines; '木자르기' is written three times across a red band. The names of the seven acts are at the top; at the bottom, Saturday 10 October 2026, 2 pm, free entry, in front of the Pungcheon-ri village hall](/images/funding/mok-jareugi/poster-20260929.webp)

Yahoyahodan is putting on Mok-jareugi, a concert in front of the village hall in Pungcheon-ri (풍천리) on 10 October. Seven acts will sing in front of a Korean pine forest that is due to be felled. The concert is a way of standing beside the residents who have opposed the construction of the Pungcheon-ri pumped-storage power plant. It follows "Pungcheon-ri, Before It's Cut Down" in front of Cheong Wa Dae on 1 August and the village feast in Pungcheon-ri's pine village on 5 September.

- When: Saturday 10 October 2026, from 2 pm until about 6 pm
- Where: in front of the village hall, Pungcheon-ri, Hwachon-myeon, Hongcheon County, Gangwon Province
- Entry: free; just come, no booking or registration needed
- Organised by: Yahoyahodan

You can also find the concert details at [Save Pungcheon-ri — Mok-jareugi](https://pungcheonri.vercel.app/concert/mok-jareugi).

## Why "mok"

The title on the poster is 木자르기. Written with the character 木 (mok, tree), it means cutting trees. Read aloud, *mok* is also the Korean word for neck, so it means cutting throats too. It looks like a pun, but in Pungcheon-ri it is not a joke.

> **Cutting our trees**
>
> **is cutting our throats**

The Korean pines are this village's income. The forest holds water and sends it down to the fields, and 51 households live below it. Felling the trees does not just change the view. It decides whether people can go on living here. That is why the residents have held on for eight years.

And now the felling has begun. Work on a relocated road has already brought down 2,256 Korean pines, and main construction has started. The saws are already past the edge of the forest.

## What is happening in Pungcheon-ri

Units 1 and 2 of the 600 MW Hongcheon pumped-storage power plant are being built in Pungcheon-ri, Hwachon-myeon, Hongcheon County, Gangwon Province. The project is run by Korea Hydro & Nuclear Power; its implementation plan was approved and announced on 29 August 2025, and main construction began in January 2026.

The construction will damage a 1,800-hectare Korean pine forest that the Korea Forest Service has named one of its "100 Best Forests." Korea Hydro & Nuclear Power plans to cut down 111,999 Korean pines, and land where 51 households lived will be flooded. Residents say they have seen long-tailed gorals in this forest. The loss is not only the trees that are cut: once the plant is in, the surviving pines will struggle to grow as they should, and once it is built, the damage to this ecosystem cannot be undone. 62% of South Korea's domestic pine nuts come from this forest.

It has been eight years. That is how long the residents have stood in the streets against the plant. Since their first rally in March 2019 they have taken to the streets more than 705 times, and their average age is about 70. Hands aged sixty to eighty have held up the placards, and seven of these residents are still on trial. In July 2024, during a sit-in at the Hongcheon County Office, residents were arrested on charges of refusing to leave (seven or eight, depending on the report). That December, summary orders imposed fines totalling ₩18,000,000, and the opposition committee asked for a formal trial.

The longer the fight goes on, trees are not the only thing the village loses. Laughter goes first.

> **"I felt like I was living like a human being. I can't remember how many years it's been since I last laughed."**
>
> — Heo Sun-i, resident, at the "Pine Valley Summer Feast," July 2025

## Another album — Fish Die Without Water

![Cover of the album Fish Die Without Water — a single fish drawn on a dark purple background, with the album title handwritten in the lower right](/images/funding/keep-singing-for-palestine/fish-album-20260930.webp "320")

This is a compilation album released in 2022 by the Noryangjin Fish Market Art Liberation Front (노량진수산시장 예술해방전선). It holds eight songs by eight acts standing in solidarity with the merchants driven out of the old Noryangjin Fish Market, each written under the influence of the merchants' stories or made to be played for them. Only 100 CDs were made, for the Seoul Record Fair, with no further pressing, and all proceeds from sales went to the merchants.

When Suhyup (the National Federation of Fisheries Cooperatives) demanded that they move to the new market, elderly women merchants who could not manage the higher rent and smaller selling space stayed in the old one. Merchants and citizens physically blocked the evictions that went on from 2017, and after most of the market was demolished in August 2019 the merchants pitched a tent on the footbridge at Noryangjin Station and held a sit-in. From October that year the musicians of the Art Liberation Front played beside them and wrote songs. The title is how the merchants feel: just as a fish dies without water, it is hard to go on living once the market, the ground of their lives, is taken away.
