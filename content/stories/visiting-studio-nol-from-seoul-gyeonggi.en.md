If you are flying into Seoul to record, or you already live somewhere in the metro area and are looking for a studio that is easy to reach by subway, this guide will walk you through how to get to Studio NOL. We are based in Yeonsinnae (연신내), a quiet neighborhood in the northwest of Seoul that happens to sit on one of the most useful subway lines in the city. Most musicians we host arrive entirely by public transit, and many tell us afterward that they were surprised at how simple it was once they had the route mapped out.

The first time foreign musicians ride Korean transit, the hardest part is usually not the train itself — it is reading the platform signs, knowing which exit to take, and trusting that the address you typed into Google Maps actually matches the one in Korean. This guide is meant to flatten that learning curve before you arrive. If you are still deciding *whether* Seoul is the right city for your project, our broader [recording in Seoul for foreign musicians](/en/stories/recording-in-seoul-for-foreign-musicians) hub covers booking, languages, and session flow — this page focuses purely on how to get to Yeonsinnae once that decision is made.

## Where Studio NOL is

Studio NOL is located in Eunpyeong-gu, in the Yeonsinnae (연신내) area of northwest Seoul. The closest subway station is **Yeonsinnae Station on Seoul Subway Line 3 (3호선)**, which is also served by Line 6. Bulgwang Station (불광역) is the next stop south and is within walking distance for some visitors as well, so either station works depending on where you are coming from.

One quick tip: Google Maps in Korea is reliable for subway and walking routes, but for door-to-door driving and the most accurate building entrance, **Naver Maps** or **Kakao Map** is what locals use. When you contact us we will send you the Korean address (한글 주소). Copy and paste that string directly into Naver Maps — pasting the Korean characters works better than trying to retype them in English. This small habit saves a lot of confusion for first-time visitors looking for the Seoul Line 3 studio.

## From central Seoul

- **From Hongdae or Sinchon** — Take Line 2 and transfer to Line 3 at a convenient interchange such as Euljiro 3-ga. Total trip is usually about 30–40 minutes door to door.
- **From Gangnam** — You are in luck: Line 3 runs directly from Gangnam through to Yeonsinnae. No transfers needed. Plan for roughly 35–45 minutes.
- **From Jamsil** — Line 8 north to a transfer point, then Line 3. About 50–60 minutes.
- **From Itaewon or Seoul Station** — Line 6 from Itaewon to a Line 3 transfer, or Line 1 / Line 4 from Seoul Station with a single transfer to Line 3. Roughly 30–45 minutes.

For Yeonsinnae studio access, Line 3 is the backbone — once you are on it, you are coming straight to us.

## From Gyeonggi (suburbs)

- **Goyang (고양)** — You are practically a neighbor. Local buses or a short hop on Line 3 will get you here in about 20–30 minutes.
- **Bucheon (부천)** — Line 7 east, then transfer to Line 3. Plan for 50–70 minutes depending on transfers.
- **Suwon (수원)** — Line 1 north combined with a Line 3 transfer, or an express bus into the city followed by subway. Usually 60–90 minutes.
- **Seongnam (성남)** — Bundang Line into Seoul, then transfer to Line 3. Around 60–80 minutes.

If your trip involves more than one transfer, give yourself a buffer — Korean trains run on time, but transfers in big stations like Express Bus Terminal can add a few minutes of walking.

## From Incheon and the airport

If you are arriving from **Incheon International Airport (ICN)**, the simplest route is the **AREX (Airport Railroad Express)** into Seoul Station or Hongik University Station, then transfer to Line 3. Total travel time is typically around 90 minutes including transfers and walking. Trains run frequently and the signage is fully bilingual, so this is the route we recommend even for jet-lagged first-time visitors.

From **Incheon city** itself, you have options: subway via Line 1 and a transfer to Line 3, or an intercity bus into Seoul followed by a short subway leg. Travel time varies more here, so check Naver Maps or Kakao Map the morning of your session.

## When you arrive

Yeonsinnae Station has several exits, and the right one depends on which side of the station you need. Rather than memorize an exit number now, we recommend messaging us on KakaoTalk on the day of your session — we will guide you from the gate to the studio door in real time. Most visitors find the walk from the station short and straightforward once they are pointed in the right direction.

There are plenty of cafes and casual restaurants around Yeonsinnae if you arrive early or want a bite afterward. The neighborhood is residential and feels calm compared to Hongdae or Gangnam, which many musicians appreciate before a long recording day. Parking is limited in the immediate area, so for the Studio NOL location Seoul visitors should default to public transit unless they have a specific reason to drive.

## FAQ

**Q: Do I need a Korean translation app?**
A: It helps but is not required. Studio NOL provides English support throughout your session, and Seoul subway signs, station announcements, and ticket machines are all bilingual (Korean and English, often Chinese and Japanese too). Papago or Google Translate covers any gap.

**Q: What is the best time to arrive?**
A: We recommend showing up 10–15 minutes early so you can settle in. If you can, avoid Seoul rush hour — roughly 8–9am and 6–7pm on weekdays — when Line 3 trains are crowded and transfers feel slower.

**Q: Where can I park if I drive?**
A: Street parking near the studio is limited and time-restricted. There are paid public lots within a short walk, but public transit is genuinely faster and less stressful for most visitors.

**Q: Should I just take a taxi from the airport?**
A: You can, but it is expensive — often ₩70,000 or more depending on traffic, and the ride can take longer than the train during busy hours. AREX plus Line 3 is faster, cheaper, and easier to plan.
